Choose a song, choose a lens, and hear what stays similar when the musical context changes.

This experiment adapts Jasper Lu’s Concept Lenses to Music4All’s MuQ-MuLan embeddings. Jasper’s blog post explains prompted similarity subspaces.

How a lens is made

The members of a lens become musical descriptions. MuQ-MuLan embeds those descriptions in the same 512-dimensional space as the audio. We center the normalized member vectors, compute their covariance eigenvectors and eigenvalues, and retain enough directions to explain 80% of member variance. Projecting audio into those directions gives a different way to measure similarity.

Control members, such as instrumental music without vocals or recordings without effects, participate with the same weight as other members. They are reference descriptions, not hard filters. The Prompts panel shows every description and marks the controls.

Text or sparse inversion

The text basis uses MuQ-MuLan text embeddings directly. The sparse basis uses the repository’s original Adam inversion with the L0-20 SAE checkpoint, packaged manifold prior, and prior-mean initialization: 500 optimization steps, learning rate 0.001, and prior regularization 0.0001. Each sparse code is decoded into a normalized 512-dimensional vector before the eigenbasis is computed. Audio embeddings remain unchanged.

Search any song

All 109,269 tracks are available as starting songs. The site lazily loads the normalized float32 projected embeddings for the selected fixed lens; a browser worker computes cosine neighbours over the entire corpus. The full-space top ten is precomputed for every song to avoid downloading the raw 512-dimensional corpus. Captions and original text-prompt affinities load in small shards when needed. The page does not host audio or run a model server.

The seven lenses and two bases are packaged ahead of time. Custom axes and adjustable basis construction remain available in the local research app.

Reading the map

The flat map uses t-SNE fitted to 5,000 fixed tracks in the selected search space. Other tracks are placed by interpolation from eight sample neighbours. Those positions are approximate: screen distance is not the retrieval score. The map stays stable as you change songs and reorganizes as you change lens or basis.

Colours show six unsupervised groups, without semantic names. Background dots are muted; the ten exact nearest neighbours in the selected space have full opacity. The optional density overlay starts off. Zoom closer to reveal title and artist labels beside tracks. Labels avoid overlap and stay readable as the camera moves. Hover or click any dot to open a card; its title or “Explore from here” makes it the next starting song. Cards appear only on hover or keyboard focus, or with a tap on touch screens. Dots are redrawn at native display resolution at every zoom level.

Listening through Spotify

Track buttons open one visible Spotify player and use Spotify’s supported iframe controls. Spotify determines playback availability for the listener. Music4All captions describe the dataset clip; a Spotify preview may play a different passage of the track. About Spotify embeds.

Caption highlights and similarity attributes are model estimates, not verified annotations or causal explanations. An attribute is shown when its original text embedding has high affinity to both the starting song and its neighbour.
